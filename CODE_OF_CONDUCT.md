# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are replying to is acting in good
faith and knows something you do not.

## What is expected

- Keep discussion technical and specific. "This EDID path fails closed on an
  ambiguous topology" is useful; "this is broken" is not.
- Accept that hardware reports are unfalsifiable from the outside. When someone
  says their MacBook behaves differently, that is data, not an argument.
- Be patient with people who are new to display timings, power schemes or
  Windows internals. Most contributors here are learning one of those.
- Respect a refusal. If a maintainer declines a display profile for lack of
  verification evidence, that is a safety decision, not a personal one.

## What is not acceptable

- Personal attacks, demeaning comments, or harassment of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information, including hardware identifiers
  they did not intend to share.
- Sustained disruption of discussions or deliberate bad-faith argument.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and any other project
space.

## Reporting

Report conduct problems privately to the maintainers through a GitHub Security
Advisory, which is private, or by contacting a maintainer directly. Reports are
handled confidentially.

Maintainers may edit, hide or delete contributions that break these rules, and
may block accounts that keep doing so. Enforcement aims to be proportionate: a
first misstep usually gets a warning.
